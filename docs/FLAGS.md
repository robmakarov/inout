# FLAGS — every switch in INOUT and its current default
Purpose: the current default of every URL flag / sticky setting and its measured effect. If a default moves, rewrite the row.
- This load only: append to the URL — `https://inout-kappa.vercel.app/?nativeres=1`.
- Sticky until changed: console `localStorage.setItem('inout.capture.nativeres', '1')` then reload.
- Precedence: URL > sticky > default. Only `?sourceframe=` STICKS when set from the URL.

## ON by default
| Switch | Turn it off with | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Quality step** `?qstep=` | `?qstep=540p\|720p\|1080p\|1440p\|max` | The slider above the input chips — the only quality decision (UI1, 2026-08-30). Default **1080p** (`inout.quality.step`). A CEILING binding both halves: capture asks for no more than that long edge (pixels above it are never encoded — the "save resources" saving) and the export ladder stops there (a step above what was captured could only be upscaled). Stamped on the take: a take opened from the list is capped by ITS step; a pre-UI1 take carries no step and keeps the ladder it was made under. `max` is what turns `?sourceres=` and `?sourcefps=` on — the screen's own resolution at 60 fps, nothing refused in advance. 1080p is the default because the composite is written at a 1920 long edge and the untouched export packet-copies it; a higher default would silently turn every export into a full re-render (pinned by test). |
| **The glued copy** `?glue=` | `?glue=record` | **J6, SHIPPED 2026-09-04 on Robert's ruling (robert (27), "kill the glued copy encoding and do background render while editing").** The live compositor still PAINTS the screen and camera into one picture — the recording preview and the frozen-screen warning both come from the paint — and never ENCODES it. `record` puts the second hardware encoder and its file back, unchanged. MEASURED on a screen+camera take (`npm run exp -- j6`): **3 encoders → 2 and 99.1 → 36.9 Mpx/s** (the composite was 62.2 of them), **0.32 → 0.16 MB/s written, 52 % less**, the compositor's own canvas still takes the preview over on both rungs, the take's own channels identical in kind/container/geometry/rate, and `?dead=screen` still says `screen source dead` with `Recording.lost` naming it (`npm run exp -- j6dead`). Screen-only takes are the same story from the other end (`npm run exp -- o3b`): 0.36 → 0.19 MB/s, 47 % less, the composite having been 46.1 % of everything the take wrote. WHAT IT COSTS: an UNEDITED take **with a camera** has nothing left to packet-copy, so its export renders — which is why J5 landed first and why the order was part of the ruling. Measured on the same take: the press costs **0.3 ms** off the file F16b makes at stop, against 74.5 ms with that file cleared and 101.5 ms for the control's instant packet copy. A take without a camera never had anything to lose (single generation's default rung copies the raw screen channel, not the composite). A MAX take is untouched: it has opened no compositor since 2026-08-30 and still does not. `stopStats.glue` carries the intake, the painter and the painted-frame count on every take that has no file to carry them. |
| **Single generation** `?singlegen=` | `?singlegen=off` | A take with exactly one video channel already MP4/AVC at the geometry of the export step you picked: the export copies THAT channel instead of a re-render (before J6, instead of the composite). Keeps colour (80.0 % vs 70.3 %), faster, 14–23 % more bytes. Since O3c (2026-08-29) it follows the SELECTED step — a native-res 1440p screen at the 1440p step is an instant copy, not a re-render; the panel badges such steps "Instant" and their size is the file, exact. |
| **Smart cut** `?smartcut=` | `?smartcut=0` | A trim-only edit copies packets instead of re-encoding — 12.7× faster, and the file is bit-identical to the untrimmed one. |
| **WebCodecs raw channels** `?rawcodec=` | `?rawcodec=mediarecorder` | Raw screen/camera recorded through WebCodecs instead of MediaRecorder (Robert's ruling 2026-08-26). 10× less audio-clock starvation, half the bytes at the same picture. |
| **Audio PCM tap** `?audiotap=` | `?audiotap=worklet` | Which tap carries a measured audio channel (mic / tab audio). `track` (default since A1): `MediaStreamTrackProcessor` reads PCM straight off the track — no AudioContext, no output-device clock, no render deadline to miss. `worklet`: the pre-A1 AudioContext + AudioWorklet path, unchanged, still the automatic fallback on any platform without the reader or without a sample rate on the track. Measured 2026-09-01 (`npm run exp -- tapstarve`, 180 s cells, one captured tab-audio track, every core busy): the worklet tap delivered 166.3 s and padded 13.6 s of silence for time its context never rendered; the track tap delivered 179.9 s and padded 0, and delivers SOONER (batch gap p50 3 ms vs 22.5 ms) — the sync anchor pays nothing. |
| **Audio tap buffer** `?audiobuf=` | `?audiobuf=0` (the platform default, i.e. the pre-B12 behaviour) | How much audio the track tap may hold while the reader is not looking, ms. Default **4000**. `MediaStreamTrackProcessor` does not wait for a starved reader — it DROPS what will not fit, and the platform default holds ~87 ms (32 quanta). Measured 2026-09-04 with a dosed main-thread block (`scripts/b12-audiostarve.mjs`, 45 s takes, `tapGapMs` off the tap's own chunk timestamps): at the default a 400/1500/3000 ms stall cost **20.1 / 28.6 / 32.5 s** of real audio out of 45, which the wall-clock hold then repaid as silence (19.2-32.0 s padded, card RED on audio-clock, 42 % of the take nothing). At 4000 ms the same three cells lose **1.5 s** and pad **0**, with the largest single gap 316→3, 1410→5 and 2913→5 ms. Costs ~1.5 MB of PCM per channel, bounded by the cap itself. It raises the threshold, it does not remove it: a stall longer than the buffer still drops, and only taking the reader off the main thread does (X11a). |
| **Where the PCM reader runs** `?audiotapthread=` (`/?test`: “Where the sound is read”) | `?audiotapthread=main` (the reader on the page — every take before X11a) | Which thread reads the track tap's PCM. Default **worker**. `MediaStreamTrackProcessor` does not wait for a starved reader, it DROPS (B12), so a busy page costs real audio: measured through the product with the buffer cut to 100 ms and the main thread blocked 32 s, the page reader lost **26.7 s** and the wall-clock hold repaid it as silence, the worker reader lost **1.56 s** against its own 1.53 s control. The probe behind it (`scripts/x11a-workertap.mjs`) proved the transferred stream is read where it is transferred TO and not proxied back: 20.0 s of block cost the page reader 13.8-15.3 s of a 21 s window and the worker reader nothing, worst step 3 ms. THE TRANSFER ITSELF IS THE CAPABILITY GATE — attempted in a try/catch, falling through to the page pump on any platform that refuses (Safari today), so nothing can lose a take over it. SYNC IS UNMOVED, and that is measured rather than argued: the batch carries the moment the worker completed it (`performance.timeOrigin + performance.now()`, converted with the page's own origin), and the page receives it **0.05 ms** later, worst p95 **1 ms** — in the take as `tapHandoffMs` / `tapHandoffP95Ms`. The first build had no stamp and placed the take 14 ms late. |
| **Microphone and computer sound** `?audiotracks=` (`/?test`: “Microphone and computer sound”) | `?audiotracks=flat` (one mixed soundtrack — every export before O10b) | Whether the measured audio channels are SUMMED into one track or written as one track EACH. Default **flat**, and the default export is byte-identical to before this existed. `separate` puts mic and system audio on their own tracks in the same file, named 'Microphone' and 'System audio' so a player's track menu reads in words; a player that understands one audio track plays the first one. THE GAINS DO NOT MOVE between the two — each track carries the bus gain and loudness makeup the flat mix would have applied, so summing the tracks gives the flat mix back. THREE PATHS DECLINE IT BY NAME rather than quietly doing something else: the instant copy and smart cut hand over the track the take already has, so both decline when a take has more than one audio channel (one channel is already one track, and nothing declines); and J1's chunked render concatenates into ONE audio track, so it declines too and the export renders unbroken — which costs a long take J1's chunk reuse and is the reason this is opt-in rather than the default. |
| **Composite engine v2** `?engine=` | `?engine=v1` | The worker compositor that owns its own encoder. v1 = the old MediaRecorder-on-canvas path. |
| **Native resolution** `?nativeres=` | `?nativeres=0` | Capture the screen at its own size instead of downscaling to 1080p — what makes the 1440p export step real detail instead of an upscale. Robert, 2026-08-29: "i need native resolution and 60 fps work, and not freezing, no turning it off." Capture is bounded at the LARGEST export step (2560 long edge, tied to QUALITY_TIERS by test), so nothing is captured that could never be exported. The freezing half is `?encoderbudget=`'s job. |
| **The recorder panel** `?panel=` | `?panel=0` | U1 — a Document Picture-in-Picture window that floats above every other app while a take runs: the timer, the audio ring, Stop and Pause/Continue. Its buttons make the SAME session calls the capture screen's own record button makes; there is no second way to end a take. Opened BY THE RECORD PRESS and closed with the take. The order inside that press is measured, not chosen (`node scripts/dpip-check.mjs`, Chrome 152): `requestWindow()` CONSUMES the click's transient activation and `getDisplayMedia` does NOT, so the screen request goes first and the panel second — reversed, the panel would spend the activation the screen needs and the take would die at the press. Chrome's picker survives the panel opening over it (the display promise was still pending 3 s later with the picker up). Costs the take nothing measured: a 15.2 s take with the panel open throughout graded GREEN, 10 of 10 dimensions in band, beside a `?panel=0` control of the same shape. The browser paints the title bar (34 CSS px on macOS Chrome, 0 on the other sides) with the origin string in it: no CSS reaches it, there is no icon API, and script can neither move nor resize the window. Off = exactly the app before U1, which is also what every browser without the API gets (Safari, any insecure context). |
| **The render remembers** `?chunked=` | `?chunked=0` | J1, ON since 2026-09-03. The export is rendered a grid-piece at a time (2.5 s since 2026-09-04 — see `?gop=` below) into files named by their own CONTENT — the recording, the settings, and the edit RESTRICTED TO THAT CHUNK'S OUTPUT SPAN — and the finished file is those chunks concatenated by PACKET COPY (never a re-encode: every chunk shares one encoder config and the avcC is byte-compared before a packet is written, as smart cut compares its boundary encoder). Naming a file by its content, and publishing it only when it is whole, gives three things at once: an edit costs only the chunks whose pixels moved, an UNDO costs nothing, and a killed tab resumes at the last complete chunk. The audio is NOT chunked — AAC and opus carry encoder priming, so splicing it would click at every join — it is one continuous encode under its own key that no picture edit touches, so a zoom costs ZERO audio work. MEASURED on a 900 s take, 180 chunks, production worker: a zoom in one span = **2 chunks, 2.2 s against a cold 166.0 s**; the same export again = **474 ms**; an undo = 442 ms; a background change = 180 of 180, honestly; 27,000 video packets copied and **0 frames re-encoded**; a kill at 50 % resumed in 85.2 s (exactly half) and the file demuxed to 900.075 s / 27,000 packets / 180 keyframes with its certification, identical to the control. It COSTS the first export **1.08×** (9,501 → 10,232 ms, n=3 a side through `exp nativerender --query=chunked=`) and about one output's worth of disk, capped at a quarter of the origin's quota, LRU, expiring in 24 h. Off = the unbroken render, which also stays the automatic fallback for a take with no video, a chunk cache that will not open, or an avcC that disagrees between chunks. |
| **Piece size the export is made in** `?gop=` | `?gop=5` | **2.5 s since 2026-09-04, Robert's ruling on J7's measured trade** (was 5 s). One number with two effects, which is why it has one row: it is the CHUNK GRID `?chunked=` builds the export in, and because the concatenation is a packet copy a chunk must start on a keyframe — so it is also the delivered file's keyframe cadence. Smaller grid, an edit re-encodes less; smaller grid, more keyframes in the file and one muxer finalize (59 ms, flat) per chunk file on the first press. MEASURED on a 30 s take at 1080p30 (`npm run exp -- j7`, docs/qa/j7-short-edit-floor.json): a nudged zoom keyframe costs **1758 ms at 5 s · 1443 ms at 2.5 s (−18 %) · 1341 ms at 1 s (−24 %)**, the file is **4926 · 5073 (+3 %) · 5511 KB (+12 %)**, and the first press is **5598 · 5770 (+3 %) · 6733 ms (+20 %)**. 2.5 s is the knee. Accepts 0.5-30; anything else is ignored. A chunk key carries the RESOLVED grid, so chunks made under one setting are never reused under another. |
| **Keep every colour** `?colour=` | `?colour=420` (off) | O9(b), 2026-09-04. The export encodes AV1 **profile 1 (4:4:4)** instead of the AVC 4:2:0 rung, so every pixel keeps its own colour instead of sharing one with a 2x2 block. MEASURED on a still code page at 1080p (`exp o9draw`): green kept **77.8 % → 99.3 %**, glyph chroma fringe **8.60 → 3.61**, for **268 KB → 296 KB (1.10x)**. Software-only — there is no hardware AV1 4:4:4 encoder anywhere — so it asks for `prefer-software` explicitly, PROBES `av01.1.{08M,09M,12M,13M}.08` at the real frame size, and on any machine or size that refuses it the export takes today's rung with nothing else changed and the decline goes **through the door** (`export colour rung 4:4:4 skipped`). Asking for it also makes the INSTANT and SMART-CUT paths decline by name — a packet copy cannot change 4:2:0 into 4:4:4, and a switch that silently does nothing is the defect `?cq=` and `?sourceframe=` already cost this project — so exporting an untrimmed take stops being instant. It NEVER becomes the blind-shared default: the file is for a destination we know (our own player, an internal channel). Part of the chunk key (`RenderFlagPrint.fullColour`). |

| **The frame follows the source** `?sourceframe=` | `?sourceframe=0` | ON EVERYWHERE since UI1 (2026-08-30, Robert: "no blackspace around" — he chose to move the OUTPUT, not only the preview, so the preview keeps predicting the export). | **The pressure detector** `?pressure=` | `?pressure=0` | The rate ladder reads leading signals four times a second from inside the compositor worker instead of judging `deliveredFps` (frames ALREADY lost): the video encoder's queue depth (the drop happens at 6, so depth = distance-to-loss), how long a frame sits in the encoder against the pipeline it can hold, that thread's scheduling lateness, and the synchronous cost per frame; worst signal wins and the step says which. Measured on Robert's machine 2026-09-01: the step lands 985 ms BEFORE the first lost frame (old ladder: 1972 ms AFTER it); recovery 576 ms after the load lifts (old: 3000 ms); no more climbing back INTO a load (old: down 17.0 s → up 24.0 s → down 27.0 s, all while loaded) — a take at the floor under pressure holds there. It refuses a step when the encoder is provably idle (queue 0.00/6, 15.9 ms latency, zero drops) = the SOURCE slowed, halving the rate fixes nothing. A step is still the rate only, never the size, never above the chosen step, never in `max` (ladder off there). Compute Pressure API and Long Animation Frames are NOT used: both silent in a hidden tab, which is every recording tab. RETUNED BY E2 (Robert 2026-09-02, "elastic purpose more to go up when possible than down"): `serious` now buys ONLY the unseen shed, the picture moves at `critical` alone, and only when the take's OWN signals are critical (a machine busy in another window is answered by shedding, not by halving Robert's rate) and only after the background work has already gone; the climb needs one confirming clear sample (~500 ms), doubling only for a climb that was undone; and a PROBE at 15 s climbs regardless of what any reading says, so no reading can pin a take. The delivery-floor backstop's second opinion went `fair` → `serious` — `fair` is this machine's steady state with three encoders open, not evidence. Same load, same machine, against the shipped ladder: dropped 55 → 14 frames, time at half rate 18.0 s → 11.1 s. Off = the ladder as shipped (the probe is the one thing that runs in both modes, and all it can do is give a rate back). |
| **The pre-render** `?prerender=` | `?prerender=0` | The export is made before it is asked for. TWO TRIGGERS since J5 (2026-09-04): a take whose export must RE-RENDER starts one AT STOP, while the machine is idle by definition; and an EDIT starts one 1.2 s after it settles (that half is `?bgrender=` below, and this switch turns both off). Pressing Export JOINS the job, never restarts it. Measured 2026-09-02: a 16.7 s max+camera take's render finished 13.2 s after stop and the export took 14 ms. Every miss, failure, cancel or supersession falls straight through to rendering on demand. A take whose export is already instant (screen-only max, every sub-max take) never starts one. |
| **Make the export while you edit** `?bgrender=` | `?bgrender=0` | **J5, ON since 2026-09-04 on Robert's ruling (robert (27), "kill the glued copy encoding and do background render while editing")**. The export for the edit he JUST MADE renders in the background 1.2 s after that edit settles, at background pace. It is not the pre-render robert (23) deleted a day earlier: that one rendered the whole take from zero and threw all of it away on the next edit, while this one renders into J1's content-keyed chunks, so a superseded job loses the 2.5 s chunk it was inside and every chunk it finished stays on disk. THE RULES, each unit-pinned in `core/compose/editRender.ts` (the one door): opening a take renders nothing, an undo back to the take as it opened cancels what was pending, an export that would be a packet copy or a smart cut starts nothing. MEASURED on a 30 s 1080p30 take, 12 chunks (`npm run exp -- j5`, docs/qa/j5-background-edit-render.json): an open untouched editor watched for 10 s scheduled nothing, started no job and wrote 0 chunks; the edit's render was ready 10.1 s after the edit; **the press a quiet minute later cost 9 ms against 5,856 ms cold (650x)**; the produced file is packet-for-packet identical to a cold foreground render of the same edit (900 video packets, 1,410 audio, same timestamps, same keyframes — the files differ in 10 container bytes, and so do two cold renders, because mediabunny stamps `Date.now()` into mvhd/tkhd/mdhd); and a second small edit (the zoom moved 400 ms) re-rendered **3 chunks of 12 and reused 9**. THE EDITOR DOES NOT FEEL IT (`node scripts/editor-drag-cost.mjs --j5`, real visible Chrome, 62.6 Hz ticker): drag p95 lateness **1.4 ms with nothing running -> 1.3 ms beside the render**, worst tick 2.7 -> 3.3 ms, **zero stalls over 30 ms** on either side, with the brake observed stepping the job to trickle (20 % duty) the moment the hand landed. Off = exactly J3's behaviour — nothing renders because of an edit — with the at-stop job untouched. |
| **The crash floors** `?crashfloor=` | `?crashfloor=0` | H2b, two additive fixes (docs/CRASH_BOUND.md): (1) the pending manifest — the only pointer to a take's files until stop writes the row — also goes to an IndexedDB store with a real transaction commit (`durability: 'strict'`) beside the `localStorage` copy; Chrome commits localStorage asynchronously, and `kill -9` at 2.8 / 3.3 / 4.2 s recovered NOTHING while megabytes sat on disk. (2) The first video fragment closes at 1 s instead of at the 2 s GOP (one extra keyframe per take; every later fragment stays 2 s), so a take killed young salvages PICTURE, not audio alone (audio was already on ~1 s clusters). Off = salvage byte-for-byte as H2 measured; the 2.1 s steady-state bound is unaffected either way. |
| **Clean audio resampling** `?resamp=` | `?resamp=hermite` (or the switch in `/?test`) | B13(3). The export mixes every audio channel to 48 kHz, and a channel that is not already there gets there through an interpolator. The old one was a 4-point Hermite — a smooth curve through four points, not a reconstruction filter — whose error rises 12 dB per octave: **-89.4 dB at 1 kHz, -30.8 at 8 kHz, -10.6 at 16 kHz** for 44.1k→48k, against -85.5 dB for a real resampler. ON since 2026-09-03 is a 32-tap Kaiser-windowed sinc (1024 phases, cutoff on the lower Nyquist so it also anti-aliases a 96 kHz source): -82 to -105 dB across the band, and no measurable export cost (120.9 s against 120.7 s on a real take). **IT IS INERT ON THE SHIPPED CAPTURE PATH and is NOT the fix for the noises Robert reported** — every measured channel is stored as opus, opus always decodes at 48 kHz, and the mix bus is 48 kHz, so the rates are equal and neither interpolator runs; the 44.1 kHz a tab delivers is resampled inside the opus encoder at capture time. Robert's ear found this before the code did ("c and d same to me"). It stays on because the mixer can still be handed a non-48 kHz channel (the MediaRecorder lane) and being correct there is free. Every export now prints which path its audio took. |
| **Clean audio resampling** `?resamp=` | `?resamp=hermite` (or the switch in `/?test`) | B13(3). The export mixes every audio channel to 48 kHz, and a channel that is not already there used to get there through a 4-point Hermite interpolator — a smooth curve through four points, not a reconstruction filter. Its error rises 12 dB per octave, measured on that very function for 44.1 kHz to 48 kHz: **-149.6 dB at 100 Hz, -89.4 at 1 kHz, -51.5 at 4 kHz, -30.8 at 8 kHz, -18.7 at 12 kHz, -10.6 at 16 kHz.** On music that is cymbals, sibilance and string harmonics each dragging grit behind them for the whole take — Robert, on a 124.8-minute take: "some small noises in tab audio". ON since 2026-09-03 is a 32-tap Kaiser-windowed sinc (1024 phases, cutoff on the lower Nyquist so it also anti-aliases a 96 kHz source): **-82 to -105 dB across the band, 81 dB better at 16 kHz**, and it costs nothing measurable — a real 30 s take exported in 120.9 s against 120.7 s for the old maths, 0.15 %. A channel already at 48 kHz takes the old path either way, so a take with nothing to resample is byte-identical (pinned by test). OFF puts the old maths back for an A/B on the same take; that is the only reason it is still in the tree. |
| **Main-thread lateness** `?lateness=` | `?lateness=0` | G7. Samples scheduled-vs-actual on the MAIN thread from the record press to the stop, and over the editor's first 15 s, and grades it as the report card's `lateness` dimension — the number Phase 1's "no editor stall > 30 ms" is read against (`__inoutReport()`, `__inoutEditorReport()`, `__inoutLateness(ms)`). AGENT/DEV SURFACE ONLY: nothing renders, and no capture decision reads it. THE CLOCK IS IN A WORKER because a take runs with the document HIDDEN and Chrome clamps a hidden page's timers to ~1 Hz — measured in one hidden page 2026-09-02, `setInterval(16)` read **1.2 Hz, p50 983.6 ms late**, the worker beat on that same main thread read **63.3 Hz, p50 0.0 ms** (`node scripts/g7-lateness.mjs --lanes=clamp`). The main thread does one subtraction per beat and the take keeps a histogram, not a list, so it is O(1) in the length of the take. WHAT IT COSTS, measured on the built bundle with CDP `Performance.getMetrics` (the renderer's own main-thread task time), idle page at 0.18 ms/s, 3 x 60 s windows: **+0.696 ms/s at the 250 ms capture period** — against **0.682 ms/s for a worker beat that does nothing at all** and 0.549 for a bare `setInterval(250)`. The instrument is within 0.014 ms/s of the platform's own price for waking the thread, which is the whole price: at a 16 ms period it is +4.75 ms/s, and the editor pays that deliberately for 15 s (83 ms of main thread, once, per editor open) because that is where the claim and B10 live. Attribution is free (+0.833 with it on, inside the noise). The take's own card also carries `selfCostMsPerSec`, which times the handler BODY only and reads ~10x low — the number above is the one to quote. Off = no sampling and the dimension reads `unmeasured` — which is what the cost A/B is measured against, and the only reason the switch exists; there is no user-visible behaviour on either side of it, which is why it is not a `/?test` row. `?latebeat=N` changes the sampling period (defaults: **250 ms during capture**, **16 ms in the editor**) — that is how the cost curve is measured, not a product knob. |
| **The background brake** `?bgpace=` | `?bgpace=0` | Keeps the pre-render off a live take (Robert 2026-09-01: "render can be parallel to record, it just must not fuck up ... make it elastic too"). Reads the same pressure instrument as the ladder (`?pressure=`): nothing recording → full speed; a take recording and nominal → half the wall clock, worked in 100 ms chunks; fair → a fifth; serious or critical → fully paused, before the composite and before anything of the take's; a take whose pressure cannot be read is treated as strained. Climbs back one rung at a time after a second of quiet; a job fully shed for two minutes gives up rather than sit on the memory (the export then renders on demand). **E3 (2026-09-04) PUT A DEADLINE ON BOTH ENDS OF IT, and both ends were measured on prod before they were built.** (1) THE BRAKE NOW ENDS AT THE PRESS. The pace is per JOB (`createJobPace()`), and `takePrerender` claims it: from the press on, a person is waiting, so it runs at full like every other user-visible export. Before this the claiming press was itself the throttle — the Export button lives inside the editor element carrying `onPointerDownCapture={noteEditingActivity}` — and joining a pre-render could finish LATER than having none, against F16's promise that it 'may only ever SAVE time'. Measured on prod, one machine, same take and same press moment: **press -> file 23,146 ms with the hand off the editor and 65,624 ms with a pointer moving over it (2.84x) before; 6,572 ms and 9,205 ms on the E3 build — 65.6 s -> 9.2 s**. (2) THE EDITOR-AHEAD WINDOW IS REFCOUNTED. It used to close two frames after the PREVIEW painted, which is before the timeline's lane art has decoded a single thumbnail; the filmstrips and waveforms then raced the pre-render for the same media engine. Measured on a 40 s take, four lanes: **the art landed 628 ms after the editor opened with nothing else running and 4,133 ms beside the at-stop pre-render** (654 ms of decode against 3,405 — screen filmstrip 152 -> 1,218 ms, mic waveform 52 -> 558). THREE THINGS NOW HOLD THE WINDOW (`holdEditorAhead`) and it closes on the last one: the preview, the lane art, and the export SIZE PROBE — which G7's editor card found to be the bigger collision of the two. With the pre-render running, that card read **RED, worst second 51 ms at 10.0s** until the probe held too, and **GREEN, worst second 10.8 ms, 0 of 934 samples over one frame** after, against a `?prerender=0` control of 10.3 ms. The art itself now lands in **812 ms beside the job against 604 ms with nothing running**, where the gap used to be 3,505 ms. WHAT IT COSTS THE OTHER SIDE, because F16's gate is that a pre-render may only ever SAVE time: the at-stop job for that 40 s take was **ready 9.3 s after stop** — later than it would have been at full, still long before any press, and the press itself is no longer braked at all. Off = a background render spends the machine exactly as before F16b — the positive control it was measured against — and with it off there is no brake for a claim to lift. |

|---|---|---|
| **The rate follows the source** `?sourcefps=` | `?sourcefps=1` | Since UI1 this is what the `max` step means — on there, off at every step below; the flag is the override. The frame RATE takes THE SOURCE'S OWN, up to 60. What a take may attempt is MEASURED (2026-08-30): the app times its own video encoder once at load while nothing records, and a take gets 60 fps when its pixels-per-second fit that reading (the old rule refused 60 above a 2560 long edge — a cliff at 2559/2561 a freeze fell through, and it refused 60 at exactly the resolution `?sourceres=` delivers). No safety margin: the ladder measures the take as it RUNS and steps the RATE down when the machine is busier than at load. Capture stops asking every screen for `max: 30`; both engines paint and encode at the take's rate; the export steps carry it. Only ever UP — a 24 fps webcam still records 30; a source that offers 30 is unchanged to the byte. Off because F15's gate wants one real 60 fps take judged by eye before the default moves, and 60 fps = twice the frames ≈ twice the file. A take exports at the rate it was RECORDED at. |
| **The burst absorber** `?burst=` | `?burst=0` | E2's layer two: past the steady queue bound of 6, up to a few more frames are KEPT in the encoder's own queue instead of dropped, so a 200 ms hiccup — a keyframe, a window drag, another tab starting — comes out of a buffer rather than out of the file. Bounded in BYTES and sized to the machine (24 MB at ≤8 GB, 96 MB above, never more than 4 frames): 4 frames at 1080p, 2 at 3024x1964, 2 at 4K. The strain denominator stays 6 on purpose — using the absorber IS the signal that the queue is one tick from overflowing, which is the only condition under which the picture may then step. Measured on the E2 cell: 207 frames kept that the shipped worker dropped; matched A/B on one build under `oracle:load` (4 runs each), composite stop tails 97/131 ms in band with it on, none in band with it off. Off = the shipped "drop the seventh frame". |
| **The compositor painter** `?painter=` | `webgpu` | Which backend paints the capture composite. `webgpu` (default since 2026-09-04, Robert's ruling) binds the frame's planes through `importExternalTexture` and uploads nothing: 0.416 ms a frame against 4.059 for the same composition (a 3024x1964 NV12 screen into a 1080p composite, screen draw + camera PiP, real fence, `scripts/painter-parity.mjs`) — 90 %, because WebGL2 uploads the frame once PER DRAW. `webgl2` is exactly what every take before this used and is the revert. `2d` is the floor, ~150 ms a 1080p frame. THE ONE VISIBLE DIFFERENCE: on a real NV12 screen frame WebGPU renders warm saturated colour MORE saturated (13,485 of 2,073,600 pixels differ by more than one step, worst 37, green channel identical every time, luma within 2 — the NV12→RGB conversion, not the drawing); on a synthetic source the two are identical at maxAbs 1 of 255, PSNR 110 dB. A/B in `~/Downloads/inout-o4`. A machine without WebGPU falls through to `webgl2` and then `2d` — the engine never refuses a record press. |
| **How frames get in** `?intake=` | `auto` | WHICH INTAKE FEEDS THE COMPOSITOR (P9's frame-source seam). Every stage of the v2 engine is portable except one: `MediaStreamTrackProcessor` on the MAIN thread, which only Chromium has. So that stage is a contract with three rungs behind it and everything after it is the one shipped worker. `main` = the processor on the page, each frame transferred — what every Chromium take has always done. `worker` = a CLONE of the track is transferred into the compositor worker and the processor is built there; the page never sees a frame. The only rung WebKit can use — **and it is proven on real Safari 26.6**: the worker has the processor, a track clone transfers into it, and it read 74 frames in 2.5 s while the original's MediaRecorder kept writing (125.8 KB against a 142.7 KB no-transfer control), so the transfer costs the take's other consumers nothing. **It cannot run on Chromium at all**: measured 2026-09-04 on Chrome 152 (headless and headed), the processor is on the PAGE here and in the WORKER there — exact complements — and a MediaStreamTrack refuses to transfer (`DataCloneError`). Asking for it on Chrome therefore lands on `element` and the console says so. Its own evidence needs a real WebKit and is not taken. `element` = a hidden `<video>` per source, paced by an AudioWorklet tick (the only clock a hidden tab does not throttle) and sampled with `new VideoFrame(el)` — v1's proven mechanism emitting into v2's worker, and the floor for an engine with no processor at all (Gecko). `auto` walks main → worker → element and takes the first that answers: PROBES, NEVER NAMES. Asking for a rung this machine does not have falls through rather than failing a take. A rung DECLARES what it can do (`FrameIntakeDeclaration` in core/types.ts), the console says it before the press and `__inoutReport()`'s `picture` line says it after — a silent difference between rungs is a defect. The element rung is the only one where the main thread touches pixels, and the only one with a rate ceiling (60 fps: its tick is 125 Hz). **ITS FIRST PICTURE IS LATE**: same canvas track, same page, same turn, Chrome 152 on prod — track processor 10 ms, `<video>` readable **4903 ms** (in the document or out of it makes no difference: 4800 vs 4803). That outran the 5 s never-delivered detector on a live take and the take honestly said "screen source dead ... composite unusable — unedited export will render" before resuming; the warning is true and is not suppressed. **That is Chromium's number, not the rung's**: real Safari 26.6 reads the same element at 35 ms and 114 ms and builds the frame in under a millisecond, 40-140x faster for identical code. Gecko is the engine this rung is FOR and is still unmeasured, so the sampler logs `element sampler: <kind> first picture at +Xms` — one Firefox run answers it, and the Firefox default does not move without that number. ARMING: a machine WITH a page-side processor is unchanged and always runs v2 through this seam; a machine WITHOUT one (Safari, Firefox) still records on v1 by default and reaches v2 only when a rung is asked for by name — moving that default is a change to which engine a user's take is made by, and it waits for Robert's yes and for each rung's own oracle cell on its own engine. |
| **The elastic system may spend the SIZE** `?floorres=` | `?floorres=1` | M1's last rung, and it is off on a MEASUREMENT — not on a rule. Robert corrected exactly that reading on 2026-09-03: "no rule for resolution first fps, only after that is resolution". So the size is a legitimate thing to give up; it is simply the LAST thing, after the rate has been spent. What keeps it off today is the seam. The rate rungs cost nothing but rate — one geometry, one encoder configuration, no seam. The size cannot: a raw encoder is configured once, so the size moves by closing the segment and opening the next (O16), and closing a segment means draining an encoder that is by definition behind. Measured on the rig 2026-09-03, one 2560x1440@60 take: the step DOWN, taken while the machine was critical, left a **5,047 ms** seam — five seconds of screen not in the file; the step back UP, taken after the load lifted, left **30 ms**, exactly O16's own band. The seam is the LOAD's cost, not the mechanism's, and it lands on the take that can least afford it. The same run could not show the rung earning it either: the size step landed at 19.0 s and the load lifted at 24.0 s, so "the take recovered" and "the spike ended" cannot be told apart. On, the floor's order is camera fps → screen fps → resolution; off, it stops after the two rate rungs and says it has nothing left. A size ALREADY given up is always restored either way — a flag must never strand a take at three quarters of its picture. |
| **The elastic system in max** `?floor=` | `?floor=1` | M1. OFF, and Robert's words are the reason: "MAX ELASTIC OFF UNTIL I SAY SO" (2026-09-03, after an agent read an earlier sentence of his as permission to move the default — it was not). WHAT IT IS: max opens no composite, and the composite is where the whole elastic system lived — the detector, the sampler, the ladder, the background brake — so max is the one mode with NO elasticity at all: it drops frames wherever they fall, which is the "severe lags, slideshow" take. On, max gets the SAME system, reading from the raw screen encoder's own worker instead of the compositor's: the unseen work sheds first (`noteTakePressure` has no caller at max today), then the camera's rate, then the screen's 60→30, everything back in reverse as the machine eases. Same `ladderDecision`, same warmup/settle/critical-only/own-work-only rules, same probe no reading may veto, same ledger. Measured on the rig (2560x1440@60 + camera, encoder spike): engaged 841 ms after the load, screen back at 60 fps 895 ms after it lifted, camera +3,975 ms, ONE segment, ZERO seams, audio silent tail 0, every event through the door. Armed on a calm take: no rung taken, 60 fps throughout. Off, the raw worker is not even asked to sample — no ticker, no counters, max byte-for-byte as it shipped. |
| **The resolution step** `?resstep=` | `?resstep=1` | The screen's raw recording FOLLOWS a mid-take source size change (display resolution changed, shared window resized) by segmenting — F6's pause/resume machinery without the gap: the current file closes and the next opens at the new size on the same track. Without it: the encoder is configured once and Chrome scales every later frame back to the starting size = the upscaling bug. NOT a load ladder — under pressure the RATE moves, resolution never (Robert's rule). Costs a stepped take its own-resolution export (two segments, two geometries, nothing to packet-copy as one file); the composite's instant export is unaffected (one size throughout). Off because it changes the files a take produces. |
| **Your own resolution** `?sourceres=` | `?sourceres=1` | Since UI1 this is what the `max` step means (flag = override, slider = switch). Does NOTHING unless `?nativeres=` is on — otherwise the flat 1080p cap applies and a take can carry a settings line claiming it while its screen is 1080p (found 2026-08-30); the panel greys it out and the badge marks it INERT. Adds a **Max** export step = the screen's own long edge (3024×1964 instead of stopping at 1440p) and lifts the capture ceiling to match (one constant). Appears on any take bigger than 1440p whose SCREEN is one continuous channel (a `?resstep=1` take that changed size gets no step). A camera does not remove it (a PiP never sets the frame size) — it changes DELIVERY: screen-only = the packet copy, instant; with a camera = re-render from the raw channels at that size; the panel badges which. Also stops the composite being recorded at all on those takes (2026-08-30): the second hardware encoder is the difference between 481 Mpx/s and 356 on a 3024×1964 60 fps take. Costs: no source-liveness detection and no composited preview (the raw `<video>` preview replaces it — measured on prod at ~1.8× the pixels of the compositor's 960×540 canvas, so sharper), and an export at a step SMALLER than the take re-renders instead of copying. J6 did NOT give those two back here: it changed what a compositor DOES, not which takes open one, and these takes still open none. |
| **The encoder budget** `?encoderbudget=` | **ON since 2026-08-30**; off with `?encoderbudget=0` | Robert: "non max video must stop fucking app record". The first thing it spends is the COMPOSITE, not the picture: over budget the composite is simply not recorded (a downscaled second copy of a frame the raw channel already holds; the third encoder that freezes a game tab in auto mode). Only if that is still not enough does it bound the SCREEN's capture size at arm time, before any encoder opens. A screen+camera take opens THREE encoders (raw screen, raw camera, composite); until O15 nothing added them up. Not a constant: the bound comes only from this machine's own history — a take that ran clean raises it, a rate-ladder step or a composite giving up lowers it, a take that worked outranks an older one that did not; a machine never seen to collapse has no budget at all, so nobody's first take is bounded. The re-render GPU-process crash is fixed (0f8fefe), so the cost of no composite is only that an unedited export re-renders instead of packet-copying. SINCE J6 the composite is not encoded on any take, so this rung has nothing left to spend first — it is the SCREEN's capture size or nothing, and `?glue=record` is what puts the composite back for it to drop. |
| **R128 loudness** `?loudness=` | `?loudness=r128` | Broadcast −14 LUFS normalization instead of the shipped p90 bounding. Off because R128 can only hit the target by turning takes DOWN; the shipped rule never attenuates. |

## Quality modes
| Switch | Example | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Capture quality** `?quality=` | `?quality=max` | `auto` (default): the machine is protected from the take — the rate ladder steps the frame RATE down under load and back as it eases; a rate the encoder measured itself unable to sustain is not attempted; with `?encoderbudget=1` a machine with a collapse in its history is bounded before anything opens; the SIZE never moves. `max`: nothing steps down and nothing is refused — the source's own resolution and rate for the whole take; there is no ladder in max (reachable with `?maxladder=1`, off by default); made to work by opening FEWER ENCODERS — at native resolution the composite is not recorded, so with more than one video channel the unedited export RENDERS (the price of max, paid at export time where a wait costs only time); source-liveness detection and the composited preview are off. |
