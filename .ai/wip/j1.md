- THE KEY DESCRIBES WHAT THE RENDER DRAWS, NOT WHICH KEYFRAMES EXIST. A test found this, not an
  argument: listing bracketing keyframes is exact about the wrong question — a zoom keyframe at
  40:00 becomes "the nearest keyframe before" for every later chunk, so a 120 s take planned 24
  chunks and invalidated all 24 while drawing identical pixels. Now the descriptor holds the
  RESOLVED value (`viewportAt`/`cameraPoseAt`) and collapses the constant case, which is exact:
  between one keyframe pair the value is a+(b−a)·ease(u) with ease strictly increasing, so equal
  values at two instants force a===b. Same zoom: **2 chunks of 24**.

## DO NOT
- Do not key chunks by index or by anything summarised (prerender.ts's key comment says why).
- Do not record raw keyframes in the descriptor — record the RESOLVED value; see above.
- Do not chunk the audio: AAC and opus carry encoder priming, so a splice clicks every boundary.
- Do not spawn a worker per chunk (worker start + first VideoEncoder ≈ 3.2 s, H6).
- Do not reuse one VideoChannelReader across a gap of cache hits — it decodes the gap.
- Do not reintroduce a length-conditional anything; `chunkPlan.test.ts` greps for it.
- Do not expect exact reuse across a TIME SHIFT. A cut moves later chunks onto different source
  instants and the shift is not generally a whole number of frames, so those chunks' pixels
  genuinely differ at sub-frame phase. Pure time edits never reach the render anyway (smart cut),
  and where a shift IS whole-frame the content key hits for free.
