## MEASURED
- PROBE, prod Chrome, 21 s window, 20.0 s of the main thread blocked, processor pinned to the
  platform's own 32-quantum buffer, two reps:
    reader on the main thread   delivered 3.6 / 3.9 s · gap 13.8 / 15.3 s · worst 1730 ms
    reader in the worker        delivered 21.1 / 21.1 s · gap 0.70 / 0.70 s · worst 3 ms
  0.70 s over 21 s is the SOURCE's own floor (33 ms/s, the rate B12's undosed control reads) and
  every step in it is under 3 ms, so the whole 20 s stall costs the worker path nothing.
- THROUGH THE PRODUCT, 45 s takes, buffer cut to 100 ms so only the thread can save it, 32 s blocked:
    reader=main    tapGap 26,739 ms · padded 26,000 ms · worst gap 1637 ms
    reader=worker  tapGap  1,559 ms · padded      0 ms · worst gap    3 ms  (own control 1,528 ms)
- THE ANCHOR, and this is what unblocked the task (`scripts/x11a-anchor.mjs`, 6 paired reps, both
  audio channels, 15 s takes, arms alternated inside ONE browser process):
    HAND-OFF (the worker's own stamp → the page's receipt of it): median of cell medians **0.05 ms**,
    worst p95 **1 ms**, every cell inside -0.1..0.2 ms. That is the whole of what moving the reader
    can do to the anchor, because the anchor dates sample 0 from arrival and the batch now carries
    the moment the WORKER completed it. The first build had no stamp and read +14 ms.
    Direction is structural: the worker's stamp always PRECEDES the page's receipt, so the correction
    can only ever place a take earlier, never later — and by at most a millisecond.

## WHAT COULD NOT BE MEASURED, AND WHY — DO NOT RE-RUN IT
The arm-vs-arm ANCHOR DIFFERENCE cannot be resolved by any number of takes, and the earlier
+14 ms / 88.3 → 102.1 ms reading was inside that noise rather than beside it. `rawAnchorMs` on an
idle machine, one build, one rig, 24 readings: **19 to 350 ms**. Paired Δ(worker−main) came out mean
−14.2 ms with **sd 83 (se 42)** on the mic and −9.2 ms sd 78 on system audio — pairs of +73.7, −20.7,
−124.8, +14.9. The spread is not the reader. Two sources of it were identified:
  · MACHINE LOAD. Two cells ran at 76-79 % busy (another session's rig) and read 195.6 and 340.3 ms
    against 28-85 ms quiet. The rig excludes them from the verdict and says so.
  · THE SOURCE'S OWN AUDIO CLOCK, which the product's first-sample line reports as `ctx=`. Cells come
    up with the renderer's audio clock either fresh (`ctx=0.00x s`) or already running (`ctx=12-14 s`,
    i.e. since page load), and the anchor tracks it: fresh cells read a median of ~36 ms, warm cells
    ~148 ms. A rig-side AudioContext opened at load does NOT pin it — that lever
    was built, tried and REMOVED, because a control that does not control is worse than none. WHAT IS STILL UNKNOWN: in this run all 6 main cells came up warm and
    4 of 6 worker cells came up fresh, which is either a real property of the arm or the coincidence
    it looks like — the control that answers it is `--arms=main,main` (below), and until that is run
    NO anchor-difference number from this rig means anything.
So the merge evidence is the HAND-OFF, which is the direct reading of the mechanism, not the
difference of two noisy anchors. The product-level sync check is the ORACLE's own band, run on both
arms of one build (`--query=audiotapthread=main`).

## DO NOT
- Do not read an anchor off a loaded machine, and never quote a single cell: the same arm reads
  28.7 ms and 162 ms on the same build twenty minutes apart.
- Do not compare two cells whose `ctx=` differ, and do not rebuild the rig-side AudioContext pin:
  it was tried, it does not decide the split, and the reason is in the rig's own header.
- The tap-gap floor is REAL and belongs to the source, not to a defect: ~33 ms/s of sub-6 ms steps on
  this synthetic source, present with the buffer on and off and with the dose at zero. Subtract the
  control before quoting any gap number.
- ONE HEADED RIG AT A TIME on this machine. Two of the twelve cells above were spoiled by another
  session's browser, and they are the two largest numbers in the set.
